TITLE
Square-Root Circuit Bounds for Iterated Functions

AUTHOR FIELD (PROVISIONAL; CONFIRM BEFORE SUBMISSION)
Samuel Schlesinger

KEYWORDS
Boolean circuit complexity; function iteration; functional graphs; nonuniform computation; sorting networks; permutation inversion; one-way functions

ABSTRACT (LATEX)
Let $F:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}^n$ have a Boolean circuit of size $s$, and put
$A=s+n^3$ and $B=A+\sqrt{n^3 2^nA}$.  Every fixed iterate $F^T$ has a
bounded-fan-in circuit of size $O(B)$, independently of the magnitude of
$T\geq0$, without assuming injectivity.  The construction uses sparse
checkpoints and sorting-based lookup, with every access and arithmetic gate
charged.  The circuit-size bound is nonuniform; constructing its hardwired
data can require work exponential in the state width.

For even $n$, an explicit linear-size permutation has almost all powers of
complexity $\Omega(2^{n/2}/n)$ under a uniform exponent modulo its order.
This matches the upper bound's exponential scale, but identifies no explicit
hard power; hard exponents necessarily have exponentially many binary digits.
The upper-bound construction also gives $O(B)$-gate cycle labels, transient
depth, pair-input reachability, exact first hitting times, and complete visit
descriptors, together with input-time and shared batched versions.  Exact
target-distance summaries have a local correctness equation, yielding
compact safety invariants and termination ranks with coNP verification.
Under SETH, general deterministic uniform analysis cannot save a fixed fraction of the
state-width exponent, even when the requested powered map has a tiny circuit.

Structured transitions admit stronger bounds.  A permutation differing from
a counter at $r$ states has $O(n(r+1))$-gate signed fixed powers and navigation,
and $O(n(r+m+1))$-gate iteration for an $m$-bit input time.  Its orbit chart
can be constructed in polynomial time from an explicit edit list.  An
autonomous $q$-bit controller with a $p$-bit affine payload gives
$O(2^q(q+p^2))$-gate fixed powers.  Lean checks these core circuit bounds;
further extensions state their individual formalization boundaries.
The checkpoint and block
methods have established antecedents, and historical priority of these
circuit formulations remains unresolved.
