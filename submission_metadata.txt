TITLE
Square-Root Circuit Bounds for Iterated Functions

AUTHOR FIELD (PROVISIONAL; CONFIRM BEFORE SUBMISSION)
Samuel Schlesinger

KEYWORDS
Boolean circuit complexity; function iteration; functional graphs; nonuniform computation; sorting networks; permutation inversion; one-way functions

ABSTRACT (LATEX)
Let $F:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}^n$ have a Boolean circuit of size $s$, and put
$A=s+n^3$ and $B=A+\sqrt{n^3 2^nA}$.  We establish three forms of
compression for finite deterministic dynamics.  First, every fixed
iterate $F^T$ has a bounded-fan-in circuit of size $O(B)$, independently
of the magnitude and bit length of $T\geq0$, without an injectivity
assumption.  Sparse checkpoints and sorting-based lookup give this bound,
with every lookup and arithmetic gate charged.  We also give variable-time
and shared batched versions.

Second, the same construction supports functional-graph queries.  Cycle
labels and transient depth cost $O(B)$; reachability, exact first hitting
times, and complete visit descriptors cost $O(nB)$.  Polynomial-size
transitions and target predicates therefore give
$2^{n/2}\operatorname{poly}(n)$-size circuits for navigation and eventual behavior,
including reachability with two $n$-bit inputs.

Third, compact orbit coordinates yield stronger bounds for structured
systems.  If a permutation differs from a counter at $r$ states, every
signed fixed power costs $O(n(r+1))$, and an $m$-bit input time costs
$O(n(r+m+1))$.  An explicit edit list permits polynomial-time construction
of the orbit chart, even when a single orbit visits all $2^n$ states.
Transport to other coordinates charges both encoding and decoding.
A separate structural bound gives $O(2^q(q+p^2))$ gates when $q$
autonomous control bits drive a $p$-bit affine payload.

The general bounds are nonuniform in the transition and fixed exponent;
they do not assert efficient preprocessing from a transition circuit.
For even $n$, an explicit linear-size permutation has almost all powers of complexity
$\Omega(2^{n/2}/n)$ when the exponent is uniform modulo its order.
Those hard powers require exponentially long binary exponents, and the
counting argument does not identify an explicit hard power.  Lean checks
the general bounds, graph-query circuits, lower bounds, affine-controller
bounds, and sparse-counter fixed powers; the full arc-chart compiler
remains outside that boundary.
The underlying checkpoint and block ideas have established antecedents;
historical priority for the circuit formulations remains unresolved.
